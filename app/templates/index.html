CourtListener is the free legal-research site BriefTune pulls opinions, briefs, and oral argument transcripts from. Tracking a judge just remembers who they are -- it doesn't pull anything by itself. Syncing is the actual fetch. The very first sync for a judge has no starting point yet, so BriefTune asks CourtListener for everything it has for that judge and pulls in all of it. Every sync after that only asks for opinions and oral arguments filed since the last successful sync, so it's incremental from there on -- nothing already pulled in gets re-fetched. Either way, every opinion that comes in also gets scanned for language that looks like it's quoting or discussing a brief -- that's a scan of text already pulled in, not a separate fetch, and it's what creates entries in "Flagged brief mentions" below. Click "Sync now" to run this for one judge, or "Sync all tracked judges now" to sync everyone at once. This column shows when that last happened, or "Never" if it hasn't run yet.

Affinity Score
note

These two sliders are linked and always add up to 100%, so moving one is a choice to weigh that signal more heavily and the other less. This sets how much each already-measured signal is weighted toward the score -- it doesn't measure anything itself; the actual match is calculated fresh from the text every time and shown in "Why this score" after you run an analysis. If one signal has nothing to measure (no synced judge style profile, no verified winning briefs, no firm folder text), its whole share is handed to the other signal automatically instead of going unused.

How it works

Upload your draft brief as a .txt or PDF file. It's processed locally and anonymized before analysis — any names or other identifying details you list are redacted, and Social Security numbers, phone numbers, and email addresses are always redacted automatically. Case numbers and dollar figures are left visible. From there, optionally compare it against a tracked judge's opinions and verified winning briefs, and/or your firm's own folder of winning briefs, using the weighting sliders to control how much each comparison counts.
